MALWAI CULTURAL HANDBOOK

Section 1: Kinship and Greetings
In the Malwa region of Punjab, respect for elders is paramount. An older mother or grandmother figure is typically referred to as "Bebe" (e.g., "Bebe ji"). Paternal uncles are referred to as "Chacha ji" or "Taya ji" depending on if they are younger or older than the father. When greeting an elder, one never says a casual "Hey"; the formal and respectful greeting is "Sat Sri Akal". 

Section 2: Wardrobe and Attire
Rural elderly men predominantly wear the traditional "Kurta Pajama", often in earthy or muted tones (whites, blues, browns). Western casual clothes like jeans and t-shirts are extremely rare among older generations in villages and are usually only worn by younger men or teenagers. Women often wear the Salwar Kameez with a Phulkari dupatta for formal occasions.

Section 3: Architecture and Setting
A typical rural Punjabi courtyard (vehera) in the early morning is a hub of activity. The floors are often made of baked brick or smooth packed earth. Charpais (woven beds) are common outdoor furniture. The morning light is soft and hazy due to the rural dust and wood-fire smoke from early cooking (chulha). 

Section 4: Social Norms
Public displays of affection are highly discouraged. Conversations between elders and youth are respectful, with the youth often listening more than speaking. Serving tea (chaa) is the universal gesture of hospitality when a guest arrives.
